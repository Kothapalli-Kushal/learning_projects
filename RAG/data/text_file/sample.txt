
MAPLEWOOD COMMUNITY LIBRARY: A FICTIONAL MEMBER GUIDE

1. About the Library
Maplewood Community Library is a fictional public library in the town of
Maplewood. This guide is sample data for learning retrieval-augmented
generation, also called RAG. All names, addresses, prices, and policies
in this guide are invented. The library helps residents borrow books,
use computers, study quietly, and attend community events. Its main
building is at 24 Cedar Lane, next to the town garden. The library has
two floors. The ground floor contains the welcome desk, children's
section, computer area, and community room. The upper floor contains
adult fiction, reference books, quiet study tables, and meeting rooms.

2. Opening Hours
The main library opens from 9:00 AM to 7:00 PM on Monday through
Thursday. On Friday, it opens from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM. Saturday hours
are 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM. The main library is closed on Sunday.
The outdoor return box is available at all hours, including Sundays.
Computers shut down fifteen minutes before the building closes.
The children's reading garden closes thirty minutes before sunset
or when the building closes, whichever happens first. During severe
weather, staff may close the building early and post a notice on
the front door and the library website.

3. Getting a Library Card
A library card is free for people who live in Maplewood. Adults must
bring a photo ID and proof of their current address to the welcome
desk. A recent utility bill or rental agreement counts as proof of
address. Children under sixteen need a parent or guardian to sign
their application. People who live outside Maplewood can purchase
a visitor membership for 20 dollars per year. A replacement for a
lost card costs 2 dollars. Members should report a lost card quickly
so staff can prevent other people from borrowing items with it.
Library cards expire after three years and can be renewed for free
when a resident confirms their contact information.

4. Borrowing Books and Other Items
A member may borrow up to ten physical items at one time. Ordinary
books can be borrowed for twenty-one days. Magazines can be borrowed
for seven days, and DVDs can be borrowed for five days. Reference
books marked with a red sticker must stay inside the library.
Members may borrow up to two board games as part of their ten-item
limit. Board games have a fourteen-day borrowing period. Staff check
the game pieces when a board game is returned. Borrowing a book does
not reserve a study room or give access to a paid workshop. Those
services have separate booking rules.

5. Renewals and Reservations
Ordinary books may be renewed twice if another member has not
reserved them. Each renewal adds twenty-one days from the renewal
date. Magazines and DVDs cannot be renewed. Members can renew
eligible items through their online account or at the welcome desk.
A member may place up to five reservations at once. When a reserved
item becomes available, the library sends an email notification.
The item stays on the pickup shelf for three opening days. After
that, it goes to the next person in the reservation queue.
Reservations are free, and members can cancel them at any time.

6. Late, Lost, and Damaged Items
Maplewood Community Library does not charge daily late fees for
ordinary books. However, an item more than fourteen days overdue
temporarily blocks the member from borrowing additional physical
items. Returning the overdue item removes that block. If an item
is forty-five days overdue, it is marked as lost. The member must
return the item or pay its replacement cost. A damaged item may
also require a replacement payment if it can no longer be used.
Staff review minor damage, such as a small tear, before deciding
whether a payment is necessary. Members should not repair books
with household tape because it can damage the pages further.

7. Digital Books and Audiobooks
Members with an active library card can borrow digital books and
audiobooks through the fictional MapleRead service. Each member may
have four digital loans at a time. Digital loans last fourteen days
and return automatically, so they never generate late fees. A member
can download a borrowed title for offline reading or listening.
Some popular titles have waiting lists because the library owns
a limited number of digital licenses. Digital loans are separate
from the ten-item physical borrowing limit. A member who has ten
physical items checked out can still borrow four digital titles.

8. Computers, Wi-Fi, and Printing
The ground floor has twelve public computers. Members can reserve
a computer for one hour per day. Staff may extend a session by
thirty minutes if nobody is waiting. Free Wi-Fi is available
throughout the building, and using it does not require a library
card. Black-and-white printing costs 10 cents per page. Color
printing costs 50 cents per page. Scanning documents to a USB drive
is free. The library does not sell USB drives. Files saved on public
computers are deleted when the session ends, so visitors should
save their work before logging out. Staff can explain basic printing
steps but cannot enter passwords or complete private forms for users.

9. Study Rooms and Meeting Spaces
The upper floor has three study rooms named Oak, Birch, and Pine.
Oak holds two people, Birch holds four, and Pine holds six.
Study rooms are free for members and can be booked up to seven
days in advance. Each member can book a maximum of two hours per
day. A booking is released if the member arrives more than fifteen
minutes late without contacting staff. The ground-floor community
room holds thirty people and is intended for group events. Local
nonprofit groups may use the community room for free. Other groups
pay 15 dollars per hour. Community room bookings require at least
three days of notice and approval from the events coordinator.

10. Children's Programs
Storytime takes place every Tuesday at 10:30 AM in the children's
section. It is designed for children aged three to six and lasts
forty-five minutes. A parent or guardian must remain with each
child. No registration is required for storytime. The Saturday
Building Club meets at 11:00 AM in the community room and is for
children aged seven to twelve. Building Club requires registration
because only fifteen spaces are available. All building materials
are provided. Children may photograph their creations, but the
materials must remain at the library for the next session.

11. Adult Workshops and Reading Groups
The beginner computer workshop runs on the first Wednesday of each
month from 2:00 PM to 3:30 PM. It covers using a mouse, browsing
the internet, and organizing files. The workshop is free, but
registration is required because only eight places are available.
The evening reading group meets on the third Thursday of each
month at 6:00 PM in the community room. Members discuss one novel
selected at the previous meeting. Joining the reading group is
free, and participants do not need to finish the book to attend.
A separate creative writing workshop costs 5 dollars per session
to cover printed materials and is limited to twelve participants.

12. Accessibility and Visitor Facilities
The main entrance has a ramp and automatic doors. An elevator
connects the ground floor and the upper floor. Accessible restrooms
are available on both floors. Large-print books are located beside
the adult fiction shelves. Members can ask staff for a portable
magnifier or headphones at the welcome desk. Service animals are
welcome inside the building. Bicycle racks are near the main
entrance, and the small parking lot has two accessible spaces.
Covered drinks are allowed at general reading tables, but food
and drinks must stay outside the computer area and reference room.

13. Donations and Volunteering
The library accepts clean, gently used books published within the
last ten years. It does not accept damaged books, old textbooks,
or incomplete board games. Donations can be brought to the welcome
desk on Fridays between 10:00 AM and 3:00 PM. Staff may add donated
books to the collection or sell them at a community fundraiser.
Volunteers must be at least sixteen years old and commit to two
hours per week for three months. Volunteer tasks include arranging
shelves, preparing event materials, and helping with the reading
garden. Volunteers do not handle membership payments or access
private borrowing records.

14. The Riverside Pickup Branch
Maplewood also operates a small pickup branch at 8 River Street.
The Riverside branch opens only on Tuesday and Thursday, from
12:00 PM to 6:00 PM. It has a reservation pickup shelf and an indoor
return desk, but no public computers, study rooms, or printing
service. Members choose their pickup location when placing a
reservation. Moving an available item from the main library to
Riverside usually takes two opening days. Riverside does not have
an outdoor return box, so returns there are accepted only while
the branch is open. The main library's outdoor return box remains
available at all hours.

15. Getting Help and Suggesting Books
The welcome desk handles membership questions, borrowing problems,
and general directions. The events coordinator handles community
room requests and workshop registration. Members may suggest up
to three books for purchase each month through their online
account. Staff consider demand, cost, and available shelf space
before accepting a suggestion. Suggesting a book does not
automatically reserve it. This guide does not specify holiday
closures, the number of parking spaces, or the library director's
name. Questions about those details cannot be answered from this
guide alone.
